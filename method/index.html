Looktwice is a student project for Arbiter Hacks V1, track Rethinking Waste. The Battery fire check was added during SafeSpace Hacks (November 2026). How the number is made

How impact is measured

Looktwice measures impact as mass diverted from the landfill and as greenhouse-gas emissions relative to landfilling, using the US Environmental Protection Agency Waste Reduction Model, version 16 (December 2023). For a logged decision, the app takes the WARM net emission factor for the path actually chosen — source reduction at the current mix of inputs when reuse avoids a new purchase, recycling, or composting — subtracts that material’s WARM landfilling factor, and converts the difference from metric tons of CO2e per short ton into kilograms of CO2e with the factor 1 short ton = 907.18474 kg. A negative result is reported as emissions avoided. Kilograms still count as diverted when you reuse, recycle, compost, or use a real drop-off even if WARM has no factor: plastic film, composted cardboard, clothing, ceramics, and household batteries are in that group on purpose, so the climate total cannot be inflated with a borrowed number. Food is entered on a wet-weight basis, which is how WARM states it, and the default grams are editable reference weights rather than measurements. The mile comparison divides avoided kilograms by 0.393 kg CO2e per mile, the EPA equivalency for a gasoline passenger vehicle in the November 2024 calculator update. The arithmetic is there to keep the order of operations honest: eating edible food or keeping a phone in use moves the WARM numbers more than parking the same object in the right cart, and composting mixed yard trimmings does not even win on greenhouse gases against landfilling. Diversion and climate are both shown because they are not the same claim.

The formula

kg CO2e = weightKg × (factor_path − factor_landfill) × 1000 / 907.18474

Factors are metric tons of CO2e per short ton from EPA WARM v16 management-practice emission factors (December 2023). Source reduction uses the current-mix column, not the 100% virgin column. A negative result is emissions avoided. Landfilling a material that has a WARM row is the zero line. The mile line divides an avoided total by 0.393 kg CO2e per mile (EPA GHG Equivalencies Calculator — miles driven by a gasoline passenger vehicle).

Worked examples

  • A 14.9 g aluminum can, recycled: 0.15 kg CO2e avoided. Per kilogram the recycling credit is large. Per can it is small. Both are shown so neither gets to pretend.
  • 250 g of leftovers, eaten: 1.15 kg CO2e avoided. The same plate composted: 0.18 kg CO2e avoided. Eating it is the bigger WARM credit because source reduction includes producing the food.
  • 1 kg of mixed yard trimmings, composted: 0.099 kg CO2e added vs landfill. The landfilling factor is more negative than composting because of carbon storage. The log does not flip that sign.
  • A 200 g greasy pizza box, composted where the city asks for it: Not estimated. Not estimated. WARM v16 lists composting of corrugated containers as NA. The food-waste compost factor is not borrowed for a cardboard box.

What is refused

  • No apparel factor. Carpet is not a shirt.
  • No battery factor. Alkaline and lithium cells are not WARM rows.
  • No ceramic factor. Glass is not borrowed for a mug.
  • No LDPE recycling factor. Rigid HDPE is not a plastic bag.
  • No polystyrene recycling factor for a foam clamshell.
  • No composting factor for corrugated cardboard or mixed paper. A pizza box does not inherit the food-waste number.
  • No phone-specific life-cycle assessment. A device uses the mixed-electronics row per kilogram, and the screen says so.

Weights

Defaults are reference empty weights, or wet weights for food, and every one of them can be edited before it is logged. A 14.9 g aluminum can is a long-used reference for a 12 fl oz can, and newer cans are often lighter. Food includes water because WARM’s food factors are per wet short ton. The stored entry keeps the grams you confirmed.

Battery fire check

Added for SafeSpace Hacks. Any object that might hide a battery (a vape, a power bank, earbuds, a toy that lights up, a card that plays music, shoes with lights) is asked “Does it have a battery?” before any bin rule runs.

  • Yes or Not sure: a battery drop-off, in every city. No other rule can override it. Only an explicit “No battery” lets the ordinary rule run.
  • Built-in batteries (phones, power banks, vapes, earbuds) skip the question. The answer is always a drop-off.
  • Swollen or hot: a static stop-handling card restated from FDNY/NYC311 and EPA guidance, with the emergency line. It is not an emergency service.
  • Counted separately: the log shows batteries kept out of carts as their own number. They have no WARM row, so no CO2e is claimed for them.
  • No model rewrites it: battery guidance is never sent to the optional language model.
  • Words that mean a battery (“rechargeable”, “bluetooth”, “light up”, “wireless”…) route an unknown object to the battery path instead of a cart.

Why: EPA documented 245 fires at 64 waste facilities from 2013 to 2020 caused, or likely caused, by lithium batteries, and says the real number is higher. EPA tells consumers to keep lithium-ion batteries and the devices that contain them out of household garbage and recycling bins, and to tape terminals or bag each battery.

  • NYC311 — Lithium-Ion Battery Safety (FDNY guidance)

What this is not

Not a hauler. Not a fine schedule. Not a claim that the right cart is the highest use. The waste hierarchy in this app is reuse, then the city’s stream, then landfill, and the climate math is allowed to disagree with the cart when WARM says so.
